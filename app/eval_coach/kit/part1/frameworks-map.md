# Five example categories of information and two sample frameworks

People have named categories of what a prompt needs. A prompting framework is a checklist of those names, so you do not forget one. The five categories below are the five this course uses. They are common, but they are not a complete list, and more exist. The two frameworks are samples, each with its own table. They are not recommendations.

The five categories of what a prompt needs:

1. Who the AI should act as
2. What exactly to produce
3. Background it needs
4. Limits and format
5. What a good result looks like

RTCC:

| Letter | Label | What you write there | Covers category |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | Role | Who the AI acts as | 1. Who the AI should act as |
| T | Task | What you want produced | 2. What exactly to produce |
| C | Context | The background it needs | 3. Background it needs |
| C | Constraints | The limits and the format | 4. Limits and format (also touches 5) |

RISE:

| Letter | Label | What you write there | Covers category |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | Role | Who the AI acts as | 1. Who the AI should act as |
| I | Input | The notes and background | 3. Background it needs |
| S | Steps | What to do, in order | 2. What exactly to produce |
| E | Expectation | What a good result looks like | 5. What a good result looks like (also touches 4) |

## How to read the tables

- RTCC stands for Role, Task, Context, Constraints. RISE stands for Role, Input, Steps, Expectation. These are common expansions, and other sources define the letters differently.
- Categories 4 and 5 fit only partly. RTCC puts both limits and a good result under Constraints. RISE puts both under Expectation. A letter that fits two categories shows "also touches" for the second. Each framework leans on some categories more than others.
- Both frameworks cover all five categories when they are filled in well. Covering what your task needs matters more than the letters.

## Others exist, and you can build your own

CO-STAR and RISEN are two more well-known frameworks. Search for prompting frameworks and you will find many others, and each picks its own categories.

The five categories here are only the ones this course uses. Your task may need categories that are not on this list, for example which sources to use, what to do when the AI is unsure, or examples of a good result. When no framework fits, build your own labels: start from these five and add the categories your task needs, or drop the ones it does not. What matters is that your prompt gives the AI what it needs.

## Skeletons

Fill in each label with your own words.

RTCC:

```
Role: who the AI acts as
Task: what to produce
Context: the background it needs
Constraints: limits and format
```

RISE:

```
Role: who the AI acts as
Input: your rough notes, plus the background it needs
Steps: how to work through it
Expectation: what a good result looks like
```
